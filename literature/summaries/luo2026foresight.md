## Summary (grounded; every factual sentence cites a page like (p.4), or (abstract) for metadata-only)
- Problem: trading-agent evaluation can fail at three levels: rankings on realized history may not transfer to other futures; prospective returns may not beat simple controls; a good terminal outcome may persist after adaptation has stopped (p.1).
- Method: nine auditable counterfactual worldlines branching from a common July 2026 information boundary, with time-gated observations and a common execution contract; two agent frameworks (AlphaCrafter, FactorMiner) × two backbones (gpt-5.6-terra, deepseek-v4-flash) = 36 runs to 2035-12-31 (p.1-2).
- Key results (numbers copied exactly): the retrospective ranking has median Spearman −0.80 with the nine prospective rankings; its winner stays first in 1 of 9 worldlines (p.1, p.3). Periodic equal weighting and a causal inverse-volatility rule each outperform 31 of 36 agent runs (p.1, p.3). In one high-return run the research library collapsed "while the declared ensemble persists and executed holdings converge toward equal weight" (p.1).
- Limitations: synthetic counterfactual futures generated by a deterministic generator (p.1-2); two frameworks only.
